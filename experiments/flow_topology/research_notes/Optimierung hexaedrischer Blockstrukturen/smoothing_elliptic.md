# Smoothing and elliptic / variational grid generation for multi-block hex meshes (block interfaces, singular vertices)

Scope note: the session ran about 27 tool calls. Several primary PDFs (NASA NTRS, HexOpt, Foldover-free maps) were read in full text. The Cadence/Pointwise manual returned 403, and ANSYS TurboGrid/ICEM and GridPro pages give little algorithmic detail, so the vendor claims below are thin. Where a finding rests only on a search-result snippet and not on a fetched document, it is marked "(snippet)". ## Q1: How do multiblock elliptic solvers make grid lines slope-continuous across block interfaces, and which codes do it?

### Takeaway
There are two mechanisms. (a) Interface nodes "float": they are solved as if they were interior points, using neighbour-block points as a stencil or halo (Pointwise "Floating" BC, ICEM elliptic smoothing without freeze, EAGLE-style composite smoothing). (b) The interface grid is generated first ("inside-out", edges -> faces -> blocks), and continuity is then obtained through consistent control functions (Spekreijse/NLR). Commercial tools (GridPro, TurboGrid ATM, ICEM) advertise smooth, orthogonal results across blocks but do not publish their algorithms.

### Cited Findings
- Pointwise/Fidelity elliptic solver: with "Floating" boundary conditions, boundary points float "as though they were interior grid points". Floating only applies if Pointwise can find a unique neighbour to the boundary among the grids selected for the elliptic solver, which means the adjacent block or domain must be part of the same solve. — [Pointwise manual, Boundary Conditions (snippet; page now redirects to cadence.com and returned 403)](https://pointwise.com/doc/user-manual/grid/solve/structured-domains-and-blocks/edge-attributes-tab/boundary-conditions.html)
- Kania (NASA, 1992), elliptic surface grids: Poisson per coordinate, solved by successive line over-relaxation. Control functions are "patterned after the work of Sorenson and Steger", "based on a prescribed step size and orthogonality constraint". In the example, "the left and right boundary points have been permitted to 'float' in order to provide orthogonality and the desired spacing in the vicinity of the corners". Boundary control functions are carried into the interior by transfinite interpolation with cosine blending (decay half-period of 10 points). — [Kania, Elliptic Surface Grid Generation in 3D Space, NTRS 19920015160](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19920015160/downloads/19920015160.pdf)
- The same paper names the period's reference codes: GRAPE2D (Sorenson, NASA TM 81198, 1980), EAGLE (Thompson, "A Composite Grid Generation Code for General 3D Regions", AIAA J. 26(3), 1988) and GRIDGEN 3D multiple-block (Steinbrenner, Chawner, Fouts, WRDC TR 90-3022, 1990). — [Kania NTRS 19920015160](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19920015160/downloads/19920015160.pdf)
- Spekreijse (JCP 118(1), 1995): the method is a composite of a nonlinear transfinite algebraic transformation and an elliptic transformation based on Laplace equations (Laplace-Beltrami on surfaces). The composite map obeys a nonlinear Poisson system whose control functions are "completely defined by the algebraic transformation". It is solved by Picard iteration and black-box multigrid. Multiblock generation proceeds inside-out: edges, then faces, then blocks. — [OSTI 121689](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/121689); report version: Spekreijse & Boerstoel, "Multiblock grid generation Part I: Elliptic grid generation methods for structured grids", NLR-TP-1996-338 — [NLR](https://reports.nlr.nl/items/98be9dd2-e610-4099-a68d-c48150e98a44/full)
- Why slope continuity matters: block-interface slope discontinuities can generate non-physical vortices where they cross high-gradient regions. The listed remedies are to move the grid defects into low-gradient regions or to interpolate at interfaces so that all interfaces have metric continuity. — (snippet) [TRID 287167, multiblock car Euler](https://trid.trb.org/View/287167)
- Ghost-cell approach (on the solver side): a smooth continuation of the mapping into ghost cells around each block reduces inter-block coupling to interpolating into those ghost cells. — (snippet) [McCorquodale et al., JCP 2015](https://portal.nersc.gov/project/edge_mdl/cogent_files/Papers/McCorquodale_JCP_15.pdf). Steinthorsson & Ameri use 2 ghost layers per block, which gives "a four-cell wide overlap between blocks". — [NASA CR-198419 / AIAA-95-0177](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19960007958/downloads/19960007958.pdf)
- ANSYS ICEM CFD "Smooth Hexahedral Mesh Orthogonal": the smoother solves "an elliptical differential equation" with a control function for "orthogonality and layer height of the first layer". It offers "Freeze: All Surface Boundaries / Selected Parts (number of layers frozen)", "Release Orthogonality" and "Initial Height" (these two are mutually exclusive), "Surface Fitting" (enabled by default; it "constrains boundary nodes to the true geometry surfaces"), and "Freeze Projected Nodes". — [ANSYS ICEM help](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/icm_help/iedit_smoothhexameshortho.html)
- ICEM workflow: block-topology smoothing comes before mesh smoothing, and the "geometry and its associative surfaces, curves, and points are all constraints when smoothing the block topology". — [ANSYS ICEM Smoothing Techniques](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/views/secured/corp/v251/en/icm_user/iuser_smoothtech.html)
- TurboGrid ATM Optimized topology produces high-quality meshes "with minimal effort; there is no need for control point adjustment". The algorithm (elliptic or otherwise) is not documented on the page. — [ANSYS TurboGrid 10.4.2](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v251/en/tg_user/tg_atm_chapt.html)
- GridPro (Program Development Co., CEO Peter Eiseman; it started from a NASA Glenn SBIR) "optimizes each grid so that every element is smooth and orthogonal throughout the volumetric region". v9.0 adds an "advanced block topology smoother". No algorithm is published in these sources. — [Digital Engineering, GridPro v9.0](https://www.digitalengineering247.com/article/program-development-unveils-gridpro-v9.0/cfd); [develop3d](https://develop3d.com/simulation/gridpro-v9-0-enhances-mesh-quality-for-cfd/); [HPC.mil description](https://arl.hpc.mil/software/description.html?sw=GridPro)
- Kim & Eberhardt (NASA CP 1995): an automatic multiblock generator that advances one cell layer with a Modified Advancing Front Method (predictor) and then corrects the new layer by solving elliptic equations. It produces blocks with "complete" connectivity, meaning identical shared faces. — [NTRS 19950022343](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19950022343/downloads/19950022343.pdf)
- A multiblock grid-deformation code applies an elliptic smoothing operator to block faces with sub-faces to keep smoothness and skewness. Block vertices are moved by spring analogy and interiors by TFI. — (snippet) [JSTD, VN](https://vjol.info.vn/JSTD/article/view/3453)

### Inferences
- The method that generalises (and fits a block-level Polytron/dtOO pipeline) is to solve one global elliptic/variational problem over the union of blocks. Interface nodes are treated as interior nodes with stencils assembled from both sides (Pointwise "floating", ICEM unfrozen interfaces). The alternative is inside-out generation with fixed interfaces (Spekreijse), which guarantees conformity but only gives slope continuity if the control functions are chosen consistently on both sides.
- For a TFI-initialised multiblock mesh, the cheapest generic upgrade is: unfreeze interface and block-vertex nodes, constrain only true geometry nodes (surface, curve, corner projection), and run a global Winslow/Poisson or variational sweep.

- The full Pointwise/Fidelity elliptic-solver documentation (control-function types: Laplace, Thomas-Middlecoff, Steger-Sorenson, Hilgenstock-White) could not be fetched (403). The control-function names come from my background knowledge, not a fetched source.
- No published algorithmic description was found for the GridPro or TurboGrid ATM smoothers. GridPro is commonly described in the literature as using an Eiseman-type energy/variational optimiser with free block corners, but I found no primary source for that in this session.
- The Thompson-Thames-Mastin (1974) equations and the EAGLE inter-block treatment were not verified against a fetched primary text.

## Q2: Special treatment of singular vertices (valence other than 4 in 2D, irregular edges in 3D)

### Takeaway
Classical structured elliptic solvers cannot write a single (ξ,η,ζ) stencil at a vertex where 3, 5 or 6 blocks meet. Practical approaches either (a) switch to an unstructured or local formulation at the singular node (Knupp/CUBIT unstructured Winslow, "virtual control volumes"), (b) use a variational, element-based energy (Brackbill-Saltzman, Ivanenko, Garanzha, TMOP), which is valence-agnostic because it sums per-element contributions, or (c) average or fix the singular point. I found no primary source describing a dedicated elliptic stencil for 3D irregular edges.

### Cited Findings
- Multiblock grids have singular points "where three, five or six blocks come together". Because of them "the grid system cannot be mapped to a single computational space", yet "allowing these kinds of singularities in the grid is exactly what gives the flexibility" for high-quality continuous structured grids. The grid in this NASA study was made with an unnamed commercial generator. — [Steinthorsson & Ameri, NASA CR-198419](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19960007958/downloads/19960007958.pdf)
- CUBIT Winslow: an "elliptic smoothing technique for structured and unstructured surface meshes" that solves "Laplace's equation with the independent and dependent variables interchanged". It "has been extended in CUBIT to work on unstructured meshes" and gives "smooth meshes with positive Jacobians, even on non-convex two-dimensional regions". It applies to surface meshes only. Boundary nodes are fixed by default, and the "free" option lets nodes on boundary curves move. — [CUBIT 17.04 Winslow](https://cubit.sandia.gov/files/cubit/17.04/help_manual/WebHelp/mesh_generation/mesh_modification/mesh_smoothing/winslow.htm)
- Unstructured elliptic (Winslow) smoothing with local control volumes: each node in computational space is treated as an individual "virtual control volume", which removes the need for a global logical (i,j,k) space. — (snippet) [ORNL, Virtual control volumes for 2D unstructured elliptic smoothing](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/virtual-control-volumes-for-two-dimensional-unstructured-elliptic/); [ORNL, Unstructured elliptic smoothing revisited](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/unstructured-elliptic-smoothing-revisited/)
- Garanzha et al. 2021 note that the Euler-Lagrange equations of the variational problem, after swapping dependent and independent variables, give the inverse Laplace equation Δx(u)=0. This is "often referred as to Winslow's functional, however Winslow himself has never formulated the variational problem". The first variational statement was Brackbill & Saltzman (1982). Ivanenko (1988) introduced barrier variational methods that guarantee non-degenerate grids, and Garanzha (2000) introduced hyperelastic stiffening for quasi-isometric grids. — [Foldover-free maps in 50 lines of code, arXiv 2102.03069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.03069)
- Unstructured hex smoother (IMR 2019): it relaxes an unstructured hex mesh to obtain smooth grid lines orthogonal to the boundary, smoothing the surface mesh first while recognising topological boundary edges. — (snippet) [Jimenez-Ramos et al., IMR 2019](https://internationalmeshingroundtable.com/assets/papers/2019/02-Jimenez-Ramos.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a block-structured mesh with valence ≠ 4 vertices, the generic choice is an element-wise variational energy (a Winslow-type functional summed over hex corners or sub-tets), not a finite-difference Poisson stencil in logical space. It needs no special case at singular vertices, because every vertex just receives contributions from however many incident cells it has.
- If a finite-difference elliptic solver is kept inside blocks, singular vertices and irregular edges are usually handled by excluding them from the PDE solve and updating them by local averaging or an optimisation step. This is common practice, but I have no primary citation for it (see Gaps).
- Inside blocks, slope continuity across a singular vertex is impossible in the strict sense: the grid lines meet at angles of 360°/valence. The achievable goal is equal-angle distribution and smooth size grading around the vertex.

### Gaps
- I found no fetched primary source describing how GridPro, Pointwise or ICEM move or treat singular block vertices (3 or 5+ valence) in the elliptic solve.
- I could not retrieve Knupp's 1999 paper "Winslow smoothing on two-dimensional unstructured meshes" (Eng. w. Computers) or the Knupp & Steinberg book text. Details of the local logical-space construction for arbitrary valence are not verified here.

## Q3: How boundary nodes slide along surfaces and feature curves while smoothing; G1/C1 continuity at vertices

### Takeaway
Two standard patterns exist. (1) Classical elliptic codes impose boundary orthogonality and first-cell spacing through iteratively updated Sorenson-Steger-type control functions and let boundary points float or reproject onto CAD. (2) Modern optimisation codes impose a three-level constraint hierarchy: corner nodes fixed, curve nodes confined to feature curves, face nodes free on the surface. This is done by projection or augmented-Lagrangian constraints. I found nothing published that targets explicit G1/C1 continuity at block vertices.

### Cited Findings
- Sorenson & Steger source terms are computed implicitly and corrected iteratively "until the first point away from a boundary is orthogonal to the boundary". Wall orthogonality and reduced spacing are needed to reduce errors in boundary-layer gradients. — (snippet) [Avestia MCM2019 HTFF 101](https://avestia.com/MCM2019_Proceedings/files/paper/HTFF/HTFF_101.pdf); NASA's GRAPE (Sorenson) solves Poisson equations with source terms acting as forcing functions for spacing and angle near boundaries — (snippet) [NTRS 19840005818](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19840005818/downloads/19840005818.pdf)
- Kania extends the Sorenson-Steger step-size and orthogonality constraints to surfaces in 3D. Surface curvature terms are kept in the inhomogeneous term "to preserve surface definition; there is no need for conventional surface splines". — [NTRS 19920015160](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19920015160/downloads/19920015160.pdf)
- ICEM: "Surface Fitting constrains boundary nodes to the true geometry surfaces". With "Freeze Projected Nodes", nodes "prescribed or projected to the geometry (curves or surfaces)" stay fixed. "Use Orthogonal Distance" measures first-layer height by projecting to the boundary. — [ANSYS ICEM help](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/icm_help/iedit_smoothhexameshortho.html)
- HexOpt (Tong & Zhang, arXiv 2410.11656, SIAM IMR 2025) uses augmented-Lagrangian constraints that keep surface points on the input triangle mesh: "corner points stay exactly at the corner, edge points are confined to the edges, and face points are free to move across the surface". It uses L-BFGS with Armijo line search plus "smart Laplacian smoothing" to escape surface-point local minima. Code: github.com/CMU-CBML/HexOpt. — [HexOpt PDF (par.nsf.gov)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10594596)
- Variational smoothing with sliding boundary vertices, based on treating the mesh as a deformed elastic body (electromagnetic AMR context). — (snippet) [arXiv 2311.06693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06693)
- Hex untangling with deformable boundary surfaces (2017, ULPGC group) and simultaneous untangling and smoothing of quad/hex meshes using a regularised algebraic distortion measure (2015). — (snippets) [ULPGC 10553/45226](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/handle/10553/45226?locale=en); [UPC](https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstreams/a62ebae9-8d5e-4c1a-9413-dbb56effa4f3/download)
- Many research codes simply lock the boundary. Protais's hexsmoothing ("Boundary of the model will be fixed") runs an untangling loop and then quality optimisation, for tets, pyramids, wedges and hexes. — [github fprotais/hexsmoothing](https://github.com/fprotais/hexsmoothing). Garanzha et al.'s examples also lock the boundary. — [arXiv 2102.03069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.03069)

### Inferences
- For dtOO blocks with an exact blade (blade label 7 / O-grid cut), the hierarchy corner -> curve -> surface maps naturally onto dtOO's analytic geometry. Instead of projecting to a triangulation, one can reparametrise boundary nodes by CAD parameters (u on curves, (u,v) on faces), which turns sliding into an unconstrained problem in parameter space.
- Near-wall orthogonality has two routes. In PDE codes, Sorenson-Steger/Hilgenstock-type iterated control functions provide it. In variational codes, an orthogonality or target-shape term (TMOP target matrices with prescribed first-layer height) is the analogue.

### Gaps
- I found no source that explicitly formulates G1/C1 continuity of grid lines through block vertices as a constraint. In practice it seems to emerge from floating interfaces plus a global smooth energy, but that is not documented in a fetched source.

## Q4: Recent (2015–2026) results on harmonic/Winslow-based hex optimisation, untangling, convergence/robustness; differentiable formulations

### Takeaway
The strongest recent theory-backed method is Garanzha et al. 2021 ("Foldover-free maps in 50 lines of code"). It uses a regularised Winslow + volume functional with a proven finite-step untangling guarantee and works for 2D and 3D (tets, and hexes through corner tets in the follow-up code). Practical hex optimisers (HexOpt 2025, TMOP/MFEM) favour smooth (differentiable) objectives with quasi-Newton or Newton solvers and constraint handling for the boundary. All of these are differentiable in the node coordinates by construction, but I found no paper that frames hex smoothing as a differentiable layer inside a learning pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- Garanzha, Kaporin, Kudryavtseva, Protais, Ray, Sokolov (SIGGRAPH 2021 / ACM TOG):
  - Regularisation χ(D,ε) = (D + sqrt(ε² + D²))/2.
  - Energy terms f_ε(J) = tr(JᵀJ) / χ(det J, ε)^(2/d) (Winslow/conformal part) and g_ε(J) = (det²J + 1)/χ(det J, ε) (volume part).
  - Problem: lim_{ε→0+} argmin ∫ (f_ε + λ g_ε).
  - They prove that their Hessian approximation is positive definite (§4.1) and that their choice of ε sequence lets the minimiser reach a mesh free of inverted elements in a finite number of steps (§4.2).
  - Stopping rule: min det J > 0 and relative energy decrease < 1e-3.
  - Limitation: different local minima can exist, for example with winding number 1 vs 2 around a vertex. Both are inversion-free, but only one is globally injective, and which one is reached depends on initialisation.
  - Python and C++ code is published.
  — [arXiv 2102.03069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.03069); [HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03127350)
- A gmsh merge request implements an untangler/smoother that minimises the Winslow functional with special regularisation for tangled elements (tris/tets). — (snippet) [gmsh MR 430](https://gitlab.onelab.info/gmsh/gmsh/-/merge_requests/430)
- Protais's hex/mixed smoother (untangle, then optimise) is superseded by github.com/fprotais/Mesh_optimization. It is documented in his PhD thesis "Maillage à dominante Polycube" (Univ. Lorraine, 2022). — [github fprotais/hexsmoothing](https://github.com/fprotais/hexsmoothing)
- HexOpt (2024/25) maximises a minimum mixed scaled-Jacobian/Jacobian energy, "rectified and scaled to quadratic measures", using AL + L-BFGS + Armijo. It reports eliminating self-intersections and obtaining min scaled Jacobians "surpassing the current state-of-the-art results" (edge-cone rectification, Livesu et al. 2015). In an ablation, the simpler objective failed to converge on models anc101 and isidore_horse. The minimum scaled Jacobian reaches 60–70% of its final value in about 10% of the run time. — [HexOpt](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10594596)
- HexOpt's related-work section: local LP untangling (Freitag & Plassmann 2000) has local convergence proofs but "gets trapped in local minimum when the local solution space is empty". Edge-cone rectification combines local QP with global reconciliation. — [HexOpt](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10594596)
- TMOP (Knupp; Dobrev, Kolev, Knupp et al., MFEM): per-point target matrices W split into volume, rotation, aspect ratio and skew. It handles high-order quads/hexes and supports r-adaptivity by putting discrete solution data into the targets. It has been extended to hr-adaptivity with nonconforming AMR. — [arXiv 1807.09807](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.09807); [arXiv 2010.02166](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02166)
- CUBIT recommends following untangling with condition-number smoothing, because untangled meshes often have poor shape. The condition-number smoother calls untangle automatically. — (snippet) [CUBIT untangle](https://www.sandia.gov/files/cubit/15.3/help_manual/WebHelp/mesh_generation/mesh_modification/mesh_smoothing/untangle.htm)
- 2015 simultaneous untangling and smoothing for quad/hex, based on a regularised algebraic distortion measure. — (snippet) [UPC repository](https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstreams/a62ebae9-8d5e-4c1a-9413-dbb56effa4f3/download)

### Inferences
- For a TFI-initialised multiblock hex mesh that may contain folds near the blade or singular vertices, a robust generic pipeline would be:
  1. Garanzha-style ε-continuation untangling on the hex corner tets, with geometry nodes constrained or reparametrised.
  2. A Winslow/TMOP-type smoothing pass, with interface nodes free.
  3. Optionally, a Sorenson-Steger-like near-wall term.
- Because f_ε and g_ε are smooth for ε > 0, they can be used directly in autograd frameworks, for example as a differentiable "mesh quality" loss or a post-processing layer for a learned block predictor. This is an inference: no paper found explicitly does this for hex blocks.

### Gaps
- I found no 2015–2026 paper giving convergence proofs specifically for Winslow smoothing of multiblock hex meshes with free interfaces and singular edges.
- I found no source on explicitly "differentiable" hex smoothing used inside neural or learning pipelines.
- I did not fetch Livesu et al. 2015 (edge-cone rectification) or the HexHex / IMR 2025 papers directly. They are cited only through HexOpt or snippets.
